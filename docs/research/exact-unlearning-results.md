## Unlearning

**12/12** unlearned models are bit-identical to retraining from scratch without the sample (12/12 differ from the original model).

Unlearning rewinds T - t steps and replays them, so it costs about 2(T - t) steps against T for retraining. With several epochs every sample is first used early, so here it is slower than retraining. It pays off when the deleted data arrived late in training, as in continual or fine-tuning runs.

## Memory needed to rewind exactly

| strategy | values per step | total (MiB, 64-bit values) |
|---|---:|---:|
| checkpoint every step / per-step weight deltas | 2,410 | 33.10 |
| Bennett full trace (keep every intermediate) | 180 | 2.47 |
| **error-factor witness (this work)** | **42** | **0.58** |
| error-factor witness, Elias-gamma coded | - | 0.241 |

The witness is 57x smaller than a checkpoint per step (137x once coded) and 4.3x smaller than keeping every intermediate value.

**The fair baseline is sparse checkpoints.** Integer training is deterministic, so a checkpoint every 58 steps plus forward replay also reaches any step exactly, with the same memory as the witness. For unlearning it is *faster*: reaching step t costs at most 58 replayed steps, while the witness rewinds T - t steps backward. The witness wins when you need to visit the states in reverse order (reverse-mode differentiation through training, scrubbing a run backward, auditing it step by step): 1,800 backward steps with 78,010 values stored, versus the best sqrt(T) checkpoint schedule, which stores 204,850 values (2.6x) and replays every step once more.

Erasing the witness irreversibly would cost at least 5.80e-15 J at 300 K (Landauer).

## Witness size is not a difficulty score (negative result)

- Mean coded witness per step: 1155 bits in epoch 1, 1084 bits in the last epoch.
- Spearman correlation between a sample's witness bits and whether the final model misclassifies it: -0.06 (epoch 1), 0.08 (last epoch); 32 training samples misclassified.
- The information each SGD step destroys is close to constant: coded length tracks the magnitude of every error component, not how wrong the prediction was.

## Where the trained network discards information

An inference program whose only witness is each ReLU's negative part (`reverie.lab.mlp_erasure_source`) ran over the 500 held-out samples and back again (exact: **True**).

- The ReLU layer discards 727,464 coded bits; the activations it keeps take 260,372. On average a hidden unit is zeroed on 74% of inputs.
- The argmax readout keeps one class out of logits worth 281,904 coded bits, so the ReLU layer, not the readout, is where most information leaves.
- Dead units (always zeroed): [31]; always-active (effectively linear) units: none.
- Ablating the 4 units that discard the *most* leaves 94.8% held-out accuracy; ablating the 4 that discard the *least* leaves 74.6% (unablated 94.8%).
